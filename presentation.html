So why does nobody act

Three countries. The same gap.

England

Schools have a duty not to harass or discriminate on religion or race.

But there is no legal requirement to record racist incidents at all. It was scrapped in 2012 and refused again in 2023.

United States

A school’s Title VI duty begins the moment it has notice.

But the conduct must be severe, pervasive or persistent. One incident almost never clears that bar.

Ontario, Canada

Staff must report serious student incidents to the principal.

But persistent low level harassment is never judged serious one incident at a time.

Every one of these systems acts on patterns. Nothing in a child’s life produces one.

US Department of Education guidance on racial harassment; England’s recording requirement scrapped 2012 and refused March 2023; Ontario Education Act s.300.2 (Bill 157).
ed.gov/laws-and-policy/civil-rights-laws/harassment-bullying-and-retaliation/racial-incidents-and-harassment-against-students

So I built the thing that produces one

Keep

A private place for a secondary school student to write down what happens to them, until it adds up to something a school has to act on.

No app. No account. No name, no email, no age. It works on a school library computer.

What Keep knows about Amina

Nothing at all.

  • No personal data is collected. No name, no email, no age, no school.
  • Her code is her key. Everything is encrypted in her browser before it goes anywhere, so the database holds text nobody can read.
  • Faces are blurred automatically when she adds a screenshot, and she can blur anything else herself before it saves.
  • Keep never reports on her behalf. If what she writes suggests she is in danger, it stops and offers her real help, then lets her decide.

These are the only two tables Keep has. An identifier, and text that cannot be read without her code.

Where this goes next

Out of my hands and into a school.

  • Refine it into a finished prototype, and test it end to end until the whole thing runs seamlessly.
  • Take it to my local school district, with the research behind it, and work out together how something like this gets implemented properly.
  • A district is the right level to start at. In 2025, researchers in Wales asked 78 pupils why they stopped reporting racist incidents. The answer was that schools logged it as ordinary bullying, so it disappeared. The local council took that finding and made the changes compulsory across all 57 of its schools at once. One conversation, every school in the area.
